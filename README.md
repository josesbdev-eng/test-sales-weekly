# Weekly Conversions Prediction

### Results from notebooks 11–14

CSA Havas technical exercise — exploratory analysis, temporal structure, multiple linear regression, and model comparison for weekly conversions based on advertising investment.

The goal is to estimate `Conversions` from weekly investment in two owned channels (`Spend Channel 1` and `Spend Channel 2`) and estimated competitor investment (`Spend Competitors`), while also evaluating whether calendar variables add explanatory information.

## Executive summary

- The analysis covers **200 weeks**, from **March 7, 2021** to **December 29, 2024**, with no missing values in the original file.
- Investment in the two owned channels explains approximately **89%** of the variation in conversions on the test set.
- Only **Spend Channel 1** and **Spend Channel 2** show a statistically significant linear association. **Spend Competitors** and the calendar variables are not significant after controlling for the other predictors.
- Adding `YEAR`, `MONTH`, and `WN` barely improves R² and **lowers adjusted R²**, so the three-variable model is preferred on parsimony grounds.
- Among **40 regression algorithms** evaluated with `LazyPredict`, `GradientBoostingRegressor` ranks first with **R² = 0.9881** and **RMSE = 0.5633** on the 40-week holdout.

### 13 — Linear regression

**Notebook:** `notebooks/13_model_regression.ipynb`

#### Data split

- Temporal **80/20 split** with `shuffle=False`: **160 weeks for training** (2021-03-07 to 2024-03-24) and **40 for testing** (2024-03-31 to 2024-12-29).
- Two models were fitted:
  1. **Main:** `SpendChannel1 + SpendChannel2 + SpendCompetitors`.
  2. **Extended:** the same variables plus `YEAR + MONTH + WN`.

#### Test-set metrics

| Model | R² | Adjusted R² | RMSE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Main (3 predictors) | 0.8955 | **0.8868** | 1.6707 |
| Main + time variables | 0.8967 | 0.8780 | 1.6606 |

R² increases by only 0.0013, while **adjusted R² decreases**. The calendar variables do not add enough explanatory power to justify the three additional parameters. The main model is preferred on parsimony grounds.

#### Coefficients and inference

Inference was performed with OLS (`statsmodels`) on the **160 training observations**.

| Predictor | Coefficient | Std. error | t | p-value | 95% CI | Decision (α = 0.05) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| Intercept | 3.041665 | 0.630098 | 4.827 | 3.32 × 10⁻⁶ | [1.7969, 4.2865] | Significant |
| Spend Channel 1 | 0.047208 | 0.001600 | 29.500 | 5.12 × 10⁻⁶⁵ | [0.0440, 0.0504] | Significant positive |
| Spend Channel 2 | 0.179978 | 0.010026 | 17.951 | 1.72 × 10⁻³⁹ | [0.1602, 0.1998] | Significant positive |
| Spend Competitors | −0.001043 | 0.006690 | −0.156 | 0.8763 | [−0.0143, 0.0122] | Not significant |
| YEAR | −0.048321 | 0.154190 | −0.313 | 0.7544 | [−0.3529, 0.2563] | Not significant |
| MONTH | −0.006206 | 0.106838 | −0.058 | 0.9538 | [−0.2173, 0.2049] | Not significant |
| WN | 0.002387 | 0.024310 | 0.098 | 0.9219 | [−0.0456, 0.0504] | Not significant |

**Overall F-test:** `F = 220.278`, `p = 1.297 × 10⁻⁷²`. The null hypothesis that all coefficients are zero is rejected; the model is significant overall.

#### Interpretation

- A one-unit increase in **Spend Channel 1** is associated with **+0.0472 conversions**, holding the other predictors constant.
- A one-unit increase in **Spend Channel 2** is associated with **+0.1800 conversions**, also holding the other predictors constant.
- Channel 2 has the larger marginal effect per unit invested; channel 1 has the much larger average investment volume.
- The 95% confidence intervals for both channels exclude zero.
- **Spend Competitors**, **YEAR**, **MONTH**, and **WN** show no evidence of an individual linear association after controlling for the other predictors. This does not prove that their effect is exactly zero.
- The intercept is significant, but its practical interpretation is limited because the all-predictors-zero scenario lies outside the observed range.

**Model diagnostics:** Durbin-Watson = 2.119; Jarque-Bera = 129.010 with `p = 9.68 × 10⁻²⁹`; residual skewness = −1.391; kurtosis = 6.408; condition number = 811.

## Conclusions

1. **The data are consistent:** 200 consecutive weeks, with no missing values or duplicate weeks within the observed period.
2. **The two owned investment variables are the relevant predictors.** `Spend Channel 1` (ρ = 0.801) and `Spend Channel 2` (ρ = 0.554) have the strongest associations with conversions, and both are significant in the regression.
3. **Competitor investment has no significant linear effect** after controlling for the two channels. Its descriptive correlation with conversions is weak (ρ = 0.195) and may be influenced by its relationship with channel 2.
4. **Calendar variables do not improve adjusted explanatory power.** `MONTH` and `WN` are strongly correlated (ρ = 0.941), and neither they nor `YEAR` are significant. Adjusted R² falls from 0.8868 to 0.8780 when they are included.
5. **The main linear model is the most interpretable option:** it explains about **89%** of the variation on the test set and quantifies the marginal effect of each channel.
6. **Tree-based models lead the predictive ranking.** `GradientBoostingRegressor` reaches **R² = 0.9881** and **RMSE = 0.5633**, followed by `ExtraTreesRegressor` and `HistGradientBoostingRegressor`.
7. **The 9 empty calendar weeks precede the first observation** and remain outside the observed period.

---

## Interpretation notes

- The reported metrics are computed on the **40-week holdout at the end of the series** (2024-03-31 to 2024-12-29).
- Residuals from the linear model are non-normal (Jarque-Bera `p = 9.68 × 10⁻²⁹`), so the p-values and confidence intervals should be read as approximations.
- `MONTH` and `WN` are highly correlated (ρ = 0.941), which makes their individual time coefficients difficult to interpret separately.
- `YEAR` is encoded as 1–4, imposing a linear trend across years.
- The estimated associations are correlational, not causal. `Spend Competitors` is also an estimated measure and may contain measurement error.
